## The invisible workforce

One teaspoon of healthy soil holds up to a billion bacteria and several meters of fungal strands. Each group does a specific job:

- **Bacteria** — decompose residues, fix nitrogen, cycle nutrients fast.
- **Fungi (mycorrhizae)** — extend roots 10–100×, transport phosphorus and water.
- **Protozoa** — release nitrogen locked in bacteria, feeding the root zone.
- **Actinomycetes** — decompose tough material, produce natural antibiotics.

## Why it breaks down

Heavy chemical use, bare fallow soil, and deep tillage starve these organisms. First to go are the fungi — which is why soils become compacted and water-repellent.

## How to rebuild it

1. Feed the microbes: compost, crop residue, root exudates (cover crops).
2. Inoculate where populations are gone: mycorrhizae, Bacillus, Trichoderma.
3. Minimize disturbance: reduce tillage, avoid harsh chemical cocktails.
