## Soil speaks before crops do

Healthy soil is alive — a single gram can contain billions of microorganisms working to feed your plants. When that ecosystem weakens, your crops pay the price. Here are five warning signs.

### 1. Hard, compacted soil

If water pools on the surface instead of soaking in, your soil structure is degrading. Beneficial fungi like mycorrhizae create the aggregates that keep soil loose and permeable.

### 2. Rising fertilizer needs

Needing more fertilizer every season for the same yield is a classic sign that natural nutrient cycling has collapsed. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria can restore part of that loop.

### 3. Repeated pest and disease pressure

Weak plants attract pests. A balanced microbiome outcompetes pathogens like Fusarium and Pythium before they reach your roots.

### 4. Pale, stunted growth despite watering

Chlorosis without a nutrient explanation often means roots can't uptake what's there — often due to missing phosphate-solubilizing microbes.

### 5. Crusty white or salty surface

Salt buildup indicates chemical overuse and dying microbial populations.
